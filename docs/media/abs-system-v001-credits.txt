The enlarged section is a generic conventional recirculating hydraulic ABS example. It does not model the donor Camry hybrid regenerative, blended or electrohydraulic brake architecture, exact Camry internal circuits, production packaging or measured CAD. One wheel circuit is expanded for readability; remaining vehicle circuits are shown only in installed source context. Machined display cuts are not leaks or service instructions. Colour and bar represent qualitative pressure; opposed caliper arrows represent clamp force. Fluid remains contained and does not represent air, spray or a constant high-volume loop through a closed caliper. Components, tiny displacement and control timing are enlarged/slowed for teaching.

Normal/build uses an open inlet and closed outlet. A lock tendency prompts inlet closure then outlet opening to reduce controlled wheel pressure; both closed holds; outlet shut/inlet reopened permits pressure increase. Released fluid is buffered in return storage and returned toward supply by the pump. This is one simplified conventional arrangement, not a universal topology. Real controls switch much faster. Pressure/displacement/speed curves are authored teaching values, not measured vehicle dynamics, RPM/friction data or an engineering validation.

The comparison imposes the same road motion and brake demand on two wheel illustrations. A locked wheel has no angular motion while the road continues; the controlled wheel retains rotation. It illustrates a control difference, not a stopping-distance contest. ABS helps preserve steering during hard braking but cannot create available grip or guarantee a shorter stop across surfaces.

Only one fault example is shown: an electrical control failure can make ABS assistance unavailable and illuminate a warning. This does not diagnose a sensor or a particular vehicle. The example's inlet-open/outlet-closed fallback is generic and is not a promise that base brakes are safe in every fault. Warning meaning and braking behavior depend on the vehicle and failure. Follow the vehicle's warning guidance; the warning alone cannot identify the failed part. No advice to keep driving, DIY testing/repair procedure, prices, intervals, diagnosis or repair promise is included.

Normal: pushrod force moves the primary piston; its pressure moves the secondary. Pressure cups pass compensating ports; each circuit transmits pressure to its separate brake load. A small displacement closes pad clearance, followed by contained pressure. On release, springs return pistons and reservoir communication reopens. The same illustrative pedal force and outlet loads are used in the fault application.

Fault: ONLY the secondary front pressure cup has an enlarged bypass clearance. Fluid passes around that worn cup to its low-side region, which communicates with the reservoir. The secondary rear separating seal stays intact; the two pressure circuits are not arbitrarily joined. Secondary circuit B fails to maintain pressure, and the pedal/pistons travel farther. The secondary nose reaches its physical fixed stop; primary circuit A can then retain pressure. This selected geometry does not promise safe base braking or universal tandem behavior. Possible longer/sinking pedal and reduced braking are qualified. Other hydraulic faults can cause similar symptoms; none is a diagnosis of the customer's vehicle. No DIY testing, repair procedure, driving advice, pricing or sales promise is included.

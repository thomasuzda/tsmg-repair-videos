The enlarged centrifugal pump, shaft support, mechanical seal and external drain are a GENERIC teaching example, not Toyota Camry OEM internals, CAD or drive architecture. No new belt path is invented and no claim is made that this hybrid uses the illustrated generic pump design. Pump drive, sealing, drain and bearing arrangements vary; some electric pump designs differ substantially.

The connected circuit is a tracked x-ray schematic using measured upper/lower hose centerlines and illustrative engine/radiator connections. It represents warm circulation, not every thermostat/control state. Internal paths are illustrative.

The near side of the enlarged housing is intentionally sectioned. Shaft, impeller hub, seal faces and bearing races remain connected/supported. The matched intact/damaged seal demonstration retains the same housing, drive/flow settings and camera. A small enlarged seal-face damage channel connects wet coolant to an air/drain space before the dry bearings. The external drain is a genuine opening in this example. The impeller moves coolant outward; the pump is not depicted as a heat source or positive-displacement device.

Blue coolant packets, local wet film and drops illustrate travel and leakage, not measured flow, drop dimensions, coolant chemistry/color specifications or a failure threshold. Reservoir lowering illustrates accumulated loss, not a real-time leak rate. Red waves indicate possible engine heat from reduced cooling, not smoke or a diagnosis. A small trace alone does not establish pump failure. Persistent/significant leakage is the selected fault example; hoses, gaskets and other joints can also leak. Possible overheating or coolant loss does not identify the cause by itself. No repair/testing steps, service intervals, prices, promises or certification.
